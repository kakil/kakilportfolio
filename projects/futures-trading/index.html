Trading Operating System

Classification before execution.

The operating system is designed around a simple idea: do not start with an entry. Start by identifying what kind of market is actually present, then allow only the playbook that belongs to that state.

The live decision sequence is Location → Reaction → Acceptance → Continuation. Higher-timeframe and 5-minute structure provide permission; lower-timeframe behavior provides timing.

Campaign

Campaign Day

Liquidity engagement, displacement, structure change, and acceptance away from equilibrium create the highest-priority expansion environment. Campaign playbooks override lower-priority modes.

Trend

Trend Day / TCM

A clean 5-minute 21 EMA trend, shallow corrective pullbacks, limited overlap, and no repeated return to EQ authorize trend-continuation execution.

Intraday flow

Directional but incomplete

When directional movement exists without full Campaign or TCM confirmation, participation is reduced and more mechanical. The goal is controlled participation, not aggressive prediction.

Protect capital

Balance / No Trade

Repeated EQ interaction, EMA/VWAP compression, overlap, two-sided wicks, and unclear direction are treated as information to stand down rather than invitations to force a trade.

Operating principle

Expansion pays. Balance protects.

The system is intentionally selective. The objective is not to predict every turn, catch every move, or trade all day. The objective is correct classification, one clean execution when the environment is authorized, and disciplined capital protection when it is not.

Indicator engineering

Kit Decision Engine

KDE is a decision-support system, not an autonomous trading signal. Its job is to make state, authority, structure, balance, value, continuation, risk, and execution context easier to read consistently.

State

Market classification

KDE surfaces Campaign, Trend, Intraday Flow, Balance/Transition, directional authority, EQ behavior, session context, and continuation state.

Execution

Evidence sequence

The setup tracker organizes evidence from location and reaction through 5-minute structure, authority, 1-minute VALUE, REASSERT, risk/runway, and READY.

Engineering

Regression first

Every material change is isolated, compiled, tested against protected behavior, documented, and frozen before the next state-machine change begins.

Current release history

Preserve what works before changing what does not.

The Phase 3 regression baseline closed with 8 of 8 protected tests passing. KDE v6.3.25 was imported and cryptographically verified, v6.3.26 removed 22 obsolete settings without changing intended trading behavior, and the current development work isolates stale READY-permission expiration as its own release.

Research & backtesting

Move from chart intuition to evidence.

The research track is designed to test specific claims about market state, authority, pullback quality, continuation, trade management, and PA-account constraints without pretending a few memorable trades prove a rule.

Hypothesis

Define the question first

Each study starts with a falsifiable question, exact setup definition, data window, session rules, entry/exit assumptions, and the result that would reject the idea.

Method

Reproducible testing

Data provenance, contract handling, costs, slippage, sample size, parameter changes, limitations, and out-of-sample or walk-forward logic are recorded where applicable.

Evidence

Keep failed studies

Failed ideas stay in the record. The goal is to improve the decision system, not to create a clean-looking history that hides what did not work.

Research themes

Questions the lab is built to investigate.

Examples include balance-to-expansion transitions, 5m/15m EMA authority, acceptance versus rejection, corrective versus reversing pullbacks, VALUE and REASSERT behavior, continuation persistence, protected-structure management, late-entry degradation, and the interaction between trading edge and funded-account risk constraints.

Public / private boundary

Share the learning without publishing the entire edge.

Public material can include system concepts, selected research, architecture, tools, demos, testing lessons, sanitized examples, and Quant Finance notes. Raw account information, credentials, unredacted trade records, licensed market data, proprietary source code, unpublished thresholds, and high-value internal research remain private by default.

The goal is a useful public lab — not a real-time signal room and not a mirror of the private control repository.
